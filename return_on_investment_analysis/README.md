# Return on Investment: Federal School Mental Health Workforce Pipeline

A two-year longitudinal analysis of federal grant programs funding school mental health workforce development across the United States.

---

## The Question

The U.S. faces a persistent school mental health workforce shortage. The national student-to-school-psychologist ratio exceeds 1,000:1 — more than double the recommended 500:1 ceiling. Two federal grant programs attempt to close this gap from opposite ends of the pipeline:

- **MHSP** (Mental Health Service Professionals) — funds universities to train and place mental health providers in schools
- **SBMH** (School-Based Mental Health) — funds K–12 school districts and state agencies to directly hire, retain, and expand access to school mental health services

This project asks: *Did these grants deliver return on their investment — and what drove the most impact?*

## Methods

### Data
Input data are synthetic and anonymized. The schema mirrors real federal APR (Annual Performance Report) data collected via Smartsheet from grantees in the MHSP and SBMH programs. All grantee names, grant IDs, and personnel information have been replaced with fictional values. Numeric GPRA outcomes preserve the statistical structure of the original dataset.

### Merge Strategy
Year 1 data (from `APRs_Year1.xlsx`) is joined to Year 2 GPRA actuals (from `MHSP_Year2.xlsx` and `SBMH_Year2.xlsx`) via grant ID, producing a grantee-level longitudinal panel. The merge is a left join from Year 1 — grantees present only in Year 2 are excluded from trajectory analyses.

### Outcome Measures
All outcomes are drawn from **GPRA (Government Performance and Results Act)** indicators, the federal standard for grant performance accountability.

| Measure | MHSP | SBMH |
|---------|------|------|
| Providers Trained | GPRA 1a | — |
| Providers Placed | GPRA 1b | — |
| Providers Retained | GPRA 2a | GPRA 2 |
| Providers Hired | — | GPRA 1 |
| Students Reached | — | GPRA 5 |

Sentinel values (999 = not reported) are replaced with `NaN` throughout. Outlier capping is applied selectively in visualizations for readability and is noted where used.

### Key Analyses
1. **Year-over-year growth** — total program output across both years
2. **MHSP pipeline funnel** — trained → placed → retained conversion
3. **SBMH student access** — students reached and student-to-provider ratios vs. NASP benchmark
4. **Regional variation** — average outcomes per grantee by federal region
5. **DEI feature analysis** — adoption rates and association with training volume
6. **Grantee trajectories** — individual-level Y1 vs. Y2 scatter plots
7. **Hire type and LEA/SEA comparisons** — provider mix and reach by grantee type
8. **Retention-reach relationship** — correlation between Year 2 retention and student reach

## Key Findings (Preview)

- **SBMH students reached grew substantially** year-over-year, with the largest gains in the West and Southeast regions
- The majority of SBMH grantees still operate **above the NASP-recommended 500:1 student-to-psychologist ratio**, illustrating the scale of remaining need
- **Retention predicts reach**: SBMH grantees that retained more providers in Year 2 reached significantly more students — sustainability matters as much as initial hiring
- MHSP grantees with **mentorship programs for providers of color** showed higher average training volumes, suggesting equity-focused strategies may also support program scale
- **Placement remains a bottleneck** in the MHSP pipeline: not all trained providers reached school placements, pointing to a structural gap between supply and deployment
